December is traditionally a time for Advent calendars, but this year I'm doing something a little different.

Throughout December, I'll be publishing 24 technical articles covering .NET development, Azure Integration PaaS, API management and the evolving world of AI integration.

The aim is simple: 24 days of practical engineering content, exploring technologies, architectural patterns and implementation approaches that solve real-world problems.

Welcome to 24 Days of .NET and Azure.

## Why this series?

The technology landscape is evolving quickly. .NET continues to mature, organisations are investing in API-led architectures, and AI agents are introducing new requirements for how applications, services and business capabilities are exposed and consumed.

For architects and developers, this creates some interesting challenges.

How do we build resilient, maintainable .NET applications? How should we connect distributed systems? How do we govern APIs across an enterprise? And, increasingly, how do we make existing business capabilities available to AI agents without compromising security, governance or control?

These are the questions I want to explore throughout December.

This isn't intended to be another series of introductory tutorials or summaries of product documentation. Each article will focus on a practical technical subject, with implementation examples, architecture diagrams, code and considerations for taking solutions into production.

The emphasis will be on understanding not just how something works, but why you might choose one approach over another.

## Practical engineering, not just theory

A key principle throughout the series is that the content needs to be useful beyond the article itself.

Where appropriate, I'll include:

- **Working code examples** – primarily using modern C# and .NET 10.
- **Architecture diagrams** – showing how services and components interact.
- **Infrastructure as Code** – using technologies such as Bicep where relevant.
- **Security considerations** – authentication, authorisation and appropriate access controls.
- **Operational considerations** – resilience, observability, performance and cost.
- **Architectural trade-offs** – understanding when a particular approach makes sense and when it doesn't.

I'll also distinguish between documented product capabilities, implementation examples and architectural recommendations. Technology moves quickly, particularly in the AI space, so accuracy matters.

## A particular focus on APIs and AI readiness

One area I'm particularly interested in is the relationship between API management and AI.

The introduction of the Model Context Protocol (MCP) creates another way for AI applications and agents to interact with external systems and business capabilities.

However, making APIs and services available to AI agents introduces questions that go beyond simply exposing an endpoint.

How do we control access? How do we discover available capabilities? How do we manage consumption? How do we monitor activity? And how do we maintain appropriate enterprise governance?

Technologies such as Azure API Management and Azure API Center have important roles to play in this conversation.

I want to explore these subjects in detail, looking at how existing API investments can support an AI-enabled architecture without losing the security, control and governance that enterprise platforms require.
